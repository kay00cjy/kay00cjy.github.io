03 · Biomechanics & mechanical design

TruMotion Technologies

Mechanical Engineering Intern · May–June 2026 · Vancouver, BC
UBC HATCH Ventures

I worked on a motor-driven rehabilitation device, focusing on the mechanical components that carry operating loads and interface with human movement. My work combined structural analysis, biomechanical constraints, and preparation for fabrication.

Operating load
900 N
Analysis
FEA and human range-of-motion constraints
Deliverables
Redesigned components and GD&T drawings

Designing around human movement

  • Redesigned load-bearing components using finite element analysis to improve structural alignment and reduce mechanical backlash under operating loads of 900 N.
  • Defined and integrated mechanical safety limits using human range-of-motion and biomechanical constraints to prevent unsafe joint positions during rehabilitation.
  • Redesigned prototype components for manufacture, developed GD&T engineering drawings, and coordinated directly with manufacturers through fabrication.

Working on rehabilitation hardware showed me how directly biomechanics shapes design. Structural performance, safe movement, and manufacturability all had to be considered together.
