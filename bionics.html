Design Team · Mechanical Sub-Team Lead

UBC Bionic Hand

Leading mechanical development through the fourth and fifth iterations of UBC Bionic Hand's prosthetic arm, with work spanning body-powered wrist actuation, mechanical design, and integration with electrical and controls teams.

Overview

The project

UBC Bionic Hand is a student design team working to develop low-cost, functional prosthetic limbs. As Mechanical Sub-Team Lead since September 2024, I oversee a seven-member team in the design, prototyping, and manufacturing of the arm's fourth and fifth iterations — focused on improving functionality, reliability, and manufacturability compared to previous versions.

My mechanism work includes a gear-driven wrist using bevel gears and compliant linkages for controlled actuation in a fully mechanical, body-powered prosthetic arm. Torque and load analysis supported gear sizing, component strength, and the potential for future motorized iterations.

Goals & Achievements

What I set out to do — and did

Key Accomplishments ✦

  • Helped deliver a prosthetic-arm iteration ahead of schedule with full functionality through coordinated design reviews
  • Led a seven-member mechanical sub-team, defining design objectives, establishing the mechanical design framework, and managing project timelines

Process

Design journey

Phase 1 · Requirements

Defining the Design Framework

Established design objectives and requirements in collaboration with the full team. Set mechanical performance targets around range of motion, load capacity, and assembly reproducibility.

Phase 2 · Concept Design

Wrist Mechanism Research & Prototyping

Researched bevel gear configurations and compliant linkage systems. Created early CAD concepts in SolidWorks and ran initial torque analysis to evaluate feasibility.

Phase 3 · Iteration

FDM Prototyping & Testing

Iterated through multiple 3D-printed prototypes with each cycle improving dimensional accuracy, fit, and mechanical performance. Applied tolerance analysis to all critical interfaces.

Phase 4 · Integration

Cross-Functional Systems Integration

Worked with electrical and controls teams to align interfaces, ownership, and milestones. Prioritized high-risk tasks and balanced work across parallel mechanical projects.

Phase 5 · Delivery

Functional Delivery & Continued Iteration

Coordinated design reviews to deliver a functional arm iteration ahead of schedule, while continuing mechanical development through the fourth and fifth iterations.
