Design Team · Mechanical Sub-Team Lead

UBC Bionic Hand

Leading the mechanical redesign of UBC Bionic's 3rd iteration prosthetic arm — a fully mechanical, body-powered prosthetic with gear-based wrist rotation, finger actuation, and systems-level integration with the electrical team.

Overview

The project

UBC Bionic Hand is a student design team working to develop low-cost, functional prosthetic limbs. As Mechanical Sub-Team Lead since September 2024, I oversee a 6-member team in the design, prototyping, and manufacturing of the arm's third iteration — focused on improving functionality, reliability, and manufacturability compared to previous versions.

The arm is body-powered and fully mechanical, meaning no motors — actuation is achieved through harness tension and compliant linkage design. This places a premium on smart mechanical design that balances biomechanical accuracy with structural durability.

Goals & Achievements

What I set out to do — and did

Key Accomplishments ✦

  • Completed the 3rd iteration of the prosthetic arm ahead of schedule with full functionality and high-quality subsystem integration
  • Led a 6-member mechanical sub-team, defining design objectives, establishing the mechanical design framework, and managing project timelines
  • Designed and optimized PCB and battery enclosures in SolidWorks with sealed geometry for splash resistance and reliable connector access
  • Researched and prototyped a gear-based wrist rotation mechanism using bevel gears and compliant linkages for controlled, natural-feeling actuation
  • Performed gear torque analysis and load analysis to verify gear strength under applied wrist loads — ensuring structural reliability and adaptability for future motorized upgrades
  • Coordinated cross-functional integration with the electrical sub-team for smooth systems-level performance

Process

Design journey

Phase 1 · Requirements

Defining the Design Framework

Established design objectives and requirements in collaboration with the full team. Set mechanical performance targets around range of motion, load capacity, and assembly reproducibility.

Phase 2 · Concept Design

Wrist Mechanism Research & Prototyping

Researched bevel gear configurations and compliant linkage systems. Created early CAD concepts in SolidWorks and ran initial torque analysis to evaluate feasibility.

Phase 3 · Iteration

FDM Prototyping & Testing

Iterated through multiple 3D-printed prototypes with each cycle improving dimensional accuracy, fit, and mechanical performance. Applied tolerance analysis to all critical interfaces.

Phase 4 · Integration

Cross-Functional Systems Integration

Worked with the electrical sub-team to ensure mechanical and electronic subsystems interfaced correctly. Finalized enclosure geometry for PCB, battery, and connector access.

Phase 5 · Delivery

Completed Arm — Ahead of Schedule

Delivered a fully functional 3rd iteration prosthetic arm with verified performance across all mechanical subsystems.
