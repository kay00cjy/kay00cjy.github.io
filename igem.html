Overview

The project

UBC iGEM is UBC's team for the International Genetically Engineered Machine competition — a global synthetic biology challenge. As a Dry Lab Hardware member since January 2025, I work on the physical systems that enable our biological experiments: bioreactors designed to cultivate cyanobacteria under controlled conditions for research into sustainable bioprocesses.

My work spans mechanical design, circuit assembly, and experimental testing — bridging hardware engineering with biological requirements. The team competed at the iGEM Grand Jamboree, where we secured a Gold Medal among 400+ competing teams.

Methodology

Working through the DBTL cycle

Each iteration of our bioreactor was driven by a structured design–build–test–learn cycle. This structured approach let us integrate academic feedback, computational fluid dynamics analysis, and hands-on experimental results into each successive version.

D
Design

Literature reviews, CAD modeling, and performance metric definition based on cell culture requirements.

B
Build

Rapid prototyping with FDM printing, drilling, woodworking, and circuit assembly with THT soldering.

T
Test

Bacterial growth curve experiments under aseptic conditions. Structured protocols, quantitative evaluation.

L
Learn

Integrate results and feedback into the next version — improving modularity, fluid dynamics, and lighting.

Goals & Achievements

What we built and achieved

Key Contributions ✦

  • Designed bioreactors optimized for aeration, agitation, and lighting based on literature reviews and performance metrics — using CAD and rapid prototyping
  • Iterated through multiple bioreactor designs using a DBTL cycle, integrating academic feedback, computational analysis, and user testing to improve fluid dynamics and lighting uniformity
  • Cultured Cyanobacteria and Caulobacter under aseptic conditions, applying Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) techniques throughout
  • Developed an end-to-end structured testing protocol for bacterial growth curve experiments — establishing quantitative benchmarks for evaluation
  • Assembled circuitry for a low-gravity bioreactor using THT soldering, integrating rotary slip rings for reliable two-axis rotation with continuous electrical connectivity
  • Competed at the iGEM Grand Jamboree against 400+ global teams — secured a Gold Medal
