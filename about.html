My Story

How I got here

I first found a love of creating through music. As a young pianist, I enjoyed taking fundamental building blocks and making something that felt my own. Performing for patients and families at Alberta Children’s Hospital showed me that what I created could also make a difference to someone else.

That experience helped lead me to Biomedical Engineering at UBC, where I’m completing a Bachelor of Applied Science with a mechanical specialization and participating in the Applied Science Co-op Program. I’m drawn to medical devices that interact directly with the human body, especially in rehabilitation and mobility.

My internships have taken me from FEA and rehabilitation-device design at TruMotion, to manufacturing tooling and automation at Sentact, to mechanical testing at Kardium. Each has added another perspective on how a design becomes a useful, reliable product.

As Mechanical Sub-Team Lead at UBC Bionic Hand, I also work with a seven-member team to turn requirements into a prosthetic arm. Leading design reviews and coordinating across mechanical, electrical, and controls teams has taught me to consider the effect of each change on the whole system — and on the person who will use it.

Outside the Lab

Music is still part of my life, and I’m learning electric guitar. I like that both music and engineering give me room to experiment, practise, and turn an idea into something tangible.
